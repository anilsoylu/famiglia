# Growth playbooks and when they apply

Playbooks the owner collected. Each worked under conditions; check the conditions against this product's numbers before recommending it. Summaries are paraphrased; see sources.

## 1. Influencer scaling (Cal AI)
Source: "Cal AI on how to scale with influencers" (interview notes, 2026; founder Zach Yadegari).

What they did:
- One early post brought ~1,000 users in two days; that proved the channel. The model was linear: each post reached viewers, a share converted.
- Built the roster month by month: founder's own TikToks first, then ~10 creators on retainer (4 posts/month each), growing to ~250 on monthly retainers.
- Outreach at volume: ~1 reply per 100 messages at first; email beat DMs before social proof; "paid promo?" in the DM preview; assistants ran outreach under platform DM limits.
- Paid fixed retainers, priced from predicted CPM, so view revenue exceeded cost. Negotiated against results; asked creators to keep posting until they hit them.
- Bought tight communities, not raw reach.
- Creators owned the video; the brand controlled the product moment: the on-camera "aha" was scanning food. Creators often did not even name the app.
- Tracked every post: custom App Store Connect campaign links per creator, staggered posting days, watched the 24-hour download feed, modeled by niche/gender/format; killed bad creative fast, reused winners as paid ads.
- When influencers plateaued (~$2M/month), added performance ads; a big sponsorship looked unprofitable at first and paid back later through credibility.

Preconditions to check before recommending it:
1. **Revenue per install > cost per install.** Compute lifetime revenue / installs (via signup and conversion rates). If it is below a realistic CPI for the category, every paid install loses money; fix activation and conversion first.
2. **A filmable aha moment that works reliably** (scan, instant result). If that feature is broken or slow, creators can't show it.
3. **Retention that keeps paid users.** If most users leave on day 0, paid traffic falls into the same hole.
4. **Measurement in place**: per-creator campaign links and the north-star metrics, or the test can't be read.
5. **Regulated categories** (finance, health): influencer posts may count as advice or advertising; require ad disclosure and no product-specific recommendations. Flag legal review; this is not legal advice.

If the preconditions fail, recommend a small learning test only after fixing them (1–3 micro creators in tight communities, separate links, a budget cap the owner sets), with a stop rule.

## 2. "$10k/month SaaS with AI" guide
Source: Hridoy Reh (@hridoyreh), "How to build a $10,000/month SaaS with AI" (PDF guide).

Mostly for starting a web SaaS (find a painful problem, validate, study competitors' pricing/complaints/missing features, MVP, add AI where it improves the product, simple monthly/annual plans via Stripe/Paddle/etc., launch on Product Hunt/HN, content/SEO, automate marketing tasks, focus on retention). Lessons that transfer to an audit:
- **Talk to customers.** Count support messages, survey answers, interviews. If there are almost none, recommend owner-sent personal emails to paying and most active free users (why use it, what's missing, why pay or not) plus a review-theme scan. No code needed.
- **Competitor complaints are a roadmap input**: read competitors' reviews for missing features and pain.
- **Free tools and SEO** can feed a product from the web (calculators, comparison pages). Measure current web traffic before building.
- **Start narrow, get the first paying customers, then scale.** Matches the audit's usual conclusion: activation and trust before acquisition.
- AI features only where they make the product better, not to sound impressive.

## 3. Using these in the report
Put an "Acquisition and playbook fit" section with: the unit-economics calculation, which preconditions pass or fail with evidence, and either a go, a small test design, or "not now, because".
